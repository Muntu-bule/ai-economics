## Post copy (paste as-is; chart image attached) — 132 words

The Big Five all promise AI value.

McKinsey: $200–$340 billion a year to global banking. Accenture: $289 billion over three years for the top 200 banks. Deloitte: 10–15% margin uplift in five to seven years.

EY-Parthenon: 47% of banks have fully implemented generative AI.

But only 12 of the top 50 banks report any realised or projected AI value, up from 8 last year. MIT found 5% of enterprise AI pilots show measurable P&L impact.

Deployment is not value.

Potential is not proof. A maturity score counts rollouts, not dollars.

Averages hide who wins and who loses. A copilot is not an agent. Autonomy decides whether a task is assisted or eliminated.

What is Big Five AI value made of: revenue, cost removed, or hours nobody hired for? And who verified it?

—
Method note: The Big Five here are the consultancies: McKinsey, Deloitte, EY, KPMG, Accenture — not the banks. Sources — McKinsey Global Institute (2023); Accenture Banking Top Trends 2026; Deloitte, Changing the game (banking & capital markets); EY-Parthenon 2025 Generative AI in Banking survey; Evident AI Index 2026; MIT Project NANDA (2025). KPMG is not quoted: its Q3 2026 banking figure could not be verified against a primary source, so it is omitted rather than printed unverified. The firms' figures use different bases and are labelled as such — McKinsey per year globally, Accenture over three years for the top 200 banks, Deloitte as margin uplift. Scope: banking, starting with Canada's Big 5 and expanding to North America's top 10. Independent research, not investment advice.
